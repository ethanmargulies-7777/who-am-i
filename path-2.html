How I see myself.

Movies, dreams, and the fantasies I get lost in.

Int. Movie night The Truman Show

If the first version of me lives in other people's eyes, the second one lives in my head, and my head runs mostly on movies and daydreams. One movie I keep coming back to is The Truman Show. Truman Burbank grows up thinking he lives a normal life in a perfect little town, but his whole life is actually a TV show. Everyone around him, his wife, his best friend, even his parents, is an actor, and millions of people are watching him without him knowing (The Truman Show). The movie stuck with me because it's such a mind-bending realization, figuring out that your whole life is just a performance. Truman played a role and people loved it, yet he always felt like something was off in his life. I can find myself playing those same kinds of roles.

The difference is that Truman didn't know he was on a show, and most of the time I do. I know when I'm being the fun friend on purpose. I knew I was lying in those interviews. But I think the feeling is the same one Truman had, that sense that something is a little off and the life everyone's watching isn't quite the one you're actually living. And honestly, I'm not sure knowing makes it better. Truman at least had an excuse.

The other thing the movie made me think about is that Truman's whole world was built to keep him inside it. Every time he wanted to leave, something pulled him back. I don't have producers or hidden cameras, but I do have my own head, and my head is really good at building worlds I don't want to leave.

Ext. Santiago Bernabéu · Night A dream

The world my head builds most often is the future. I dream a lot. Not just at night. I mean during the day, in class, on a drive, lying in bed. I'm always imagining different futures for myself. I have a lot of dreams, but one that keeps coming back is me as a professional soccer player. It's been my dream since I was young. In it I'm playing for my favorite team, Real Madrid, and I feel free on the field. I can feel the wind blowing through my hair as I glide past a defender, and I feel the rush when I strike the ball into the back of the net and celebrate.

What I notice about that dream is how much of it is just a feeling. It's not really about trophies or money or being famous. It's about being free, and being good at something without having to think about it. In the dream I'm not performing for anybody. I'm just playing. Last spring I studied abroad in Madrid, the same city as the team I used to picture myself playing for. I was there as a student, not a player, and that's kind of the whole gap between the dream and my life in one sentence.

Int. Somewhere far away A feeling, not a picture

The dream I get lost in the most, though, is about love. It's not really a picture, it's more of a feeling. The feeling that someone is sharing my heart, that they're there with me, and that I'm comfortable and safe enough to just be myself. I imagine going around the world with someone, starting a family, and growing old with someone at my side. I find myself wishing that dream was reality, and I can stay in it for a long time. When I put the two dreams next to each other, they're kind of asking for the same thing. On the field and with that person, I get to stop acting. Maybe that's why I go back to them so much. They're the only places, even if they're imaginary, where there's no front stage.

Mind-wandering
46.9%

of our waking hours are spent thinking about something other than what we're actually doing.

(Killingsworth and Gilbert 932)

It turns out I'm not the only one living half in my head. Researchers at Harvard used a phone app to check in on thousands of people throughout their day and found that people's minds wander almost half the time, about 46.9 percent of their waking hours. Even more interesting, the mind-wandering seemed to cause unhappiness, not the other way around (Killingsworth and Gilbert 932). That makes sense to me. When I'm dreaming about the perfect future, the present always feels a little worse by comparison. It's kind of ironic that they used a phone to study it, because these days my phone is where a lot of my mind-wandering happens. Scrolling is just daydreaming that someone else wrote for you.

Eckhart Tolle would say this is just the mind doing what it always does. In "You Are Not Your Mind," he argues that we identify so much with our thoughts that we end up living in the past or the future and almost never in the now (Tolle). I don't fully agree with him. I think dreaming is part of what makes life worth anything, and I don't want to give it up. But I do think there's a risk. If the self I see in my head gets too far ahead of what's actually happening, I start living for a version of my life that doesn't exist. And sooner or later, reality shows up to check on that version.
